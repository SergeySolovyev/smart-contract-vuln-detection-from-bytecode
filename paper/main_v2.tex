\begin{abstract}
Smart-contract vulnerabilities have caused losses exceeding USD~3~billion in
decentralised finance, while most Ethereum mainnet contracts ship without
verified source~\cite{cobra,scannerstudy} and are therefore unreachable by
source-required analysers such as Slither and Mythril. We present a
lightweight \vNFeat-feature pipeline operating directly on EVM bytecode as a
Slither-consistent risk-scoring filter, and study both \emph{binary}
any-vulnerability detection and \emph{multi-label} classification over eight
vulnerability classes on \vCorpus{} Slither-labelled contracts. Duplicate
contracts are removed at the level of the compiled feature representation
rather than the raw bytecode string, which we show is the difference between
a leaking and a clean split; hyperparameters and thresholds are selected on a
validation split and the test split is read exactly once. On binary
detection, gradient boosting reaches $F_1 =$ \vBXGBFone{}
{[}\vBXGBLo, \vBXGBHi{]} at a false-negative rate of \vBXGBFnr, and a paired
bootstrap---rather than the overlap of marginal intervals---resolves its
advantage over RandomForest ($\Delta F_1 = \vDeltaFone$, 95\% CI
{[}\vDeltaLo, \vDeltaHi{]}). On multi-label classification, classical XGBoost
(\vMlXgb) outperforms all \vDlNConfigs{} configurations of a
Conv-Transformer ablation (best \vDlBest) at roughly an order of magnitude
less training compute. We attribute the negative deep-learning result to the
tabular-feature regime, where tree ensembles remain
dominant~\cite{grinsztajn2022,shwartz2022}, and we report the statistical
evidence for it with the limits that eight paired observations actually
support.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro-v2}

Decentralised finance holds tens of billions of dollars in total value
locked, and public leaderboards record cumulative losses past USD~3~billion
from smart-contract exploits since 2016~\cite{rekt}. Contracts holding these
assets are immutable after deployment, which makes pre-deployment and
pre-interaction screening the only lever available at scale.

Static analysers and symbolic executors (Slither~\cite{slither},
Mythril~\cite{mythril}, Manticore~\cite{manticore}) require Solidity source,
while a substantial fraction of mainnet-deployed contracts ship
\emph{without} verified source---their bytecode is the only universally
available artefact. We position this work as a Tier-1 pre-filter: a
bytecode-only predictor that screens every deployment cheaply and forwards
the most suspicious fraction to deep static analysis and, above that, to
symbolic exploit search.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\textbf{(i)~A bytecode-only Tier-1 pre-filter} over \vNFeat{} engineered
features, evaluated with a security-relevant metric suite (FNR, PR-AUC,
precision/recall at a validation-selected threshold).
\textbf{(ii)~A protocol that cannot flatter the result:} duplicates removed
in feature space, a three-way split with a build-time no-overlap invariant,
tuning confined to training folds, and a single read of the test split.
\textbf{(iii)~A deep comparator and an honest account of its defeat:}
\vDlNConfigs{} Conv-Transformer configurations, compared with paired tests
whose resolution limits we state explicitly rather than over-claim.

\paragraph{Relation to an earlier version of this work.}
An earlier preprint reported comparable headline numbers under a protocol we
now consider unsound: duplicates were removed on the raw bytecode string,
which left contracts that are bit-identical in feature space on both sides of
the split, and the search over hyperparameters was scored on the same data
used for reporting. The present numbers are produced under the protocol
described below (Sec.~\ref{sec:data-v2}), and we report both the corrected
results and the correction itself, because the size of the correction is
itself evidence about how much such defects matter in this domain.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:concl-v2}

A \vNFeat-feature bytecode-only classifier detects Slither-flagged
vulnerabilities on held-out contracts at $F_1 =$ \vBXGBFone{} with a
false-negative rate of \vBXGBFnr, under a protocol in which the test split
is read once and duplicates are eliminated in the representation the model
actually sees. On the eight-class task, classical gradient boosting
(\vMlXgb) beats every one of \vDlNConfigs{} deep configurations (best
\vDlBest), at roughly an order of magnitude less compute.

The methodological lesson generalises beyond this dataset. Deduplicating on
raw bytecode is insufficient whenever features are computed from a
normalised view of that bytecode: compiler metadata alone is enough to
defeat string-level deduplication while leaving the model's input identical.
We recommend that bytecode-based work report deduplication at the feature
level and publish the resulting overlap statistics, as we do here.
